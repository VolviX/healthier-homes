# Utensils & Tools

Spatulas, spoons, whisks, and prep tools touch food constantly, and the ones used in hot
pans face the added factor of heat. Because contact is usually brief, the stakes are lower
than for storage or cookware — but one common category, black plastic, has become a genuine
concern worth designing around. Material and heat tolerance are the two things to watch.

## Recommended Materials

### Stainless steel — inert and effectively permanent

Food-grade stainless steel (**18/8 or 18/10**, grade 304) is the cleanest choice for whisks,
tongs, ladles, slotted spoons, and prep tools: non-reactive, non-leaching at the brief
contact times utensils see, dishwasher-safe, and durable enough to last indefinitely. There
is no coating to wear off and nothing to migrate into food.

*What to consider:* metal will scratch non-stick coatings, so keep steel tools off coated
pans (see silicone below). That's a usage note, not a safety downside.

### Solid wood & bamboo — gentle, coating-free, naturally hygienic

Solid wood and bamboo are gentle on cookware, pleasant to use, and free of coatings or
plastics. Wood is also **naturally antibacterial**: bacteria deposited on a wooden surface
are drawn in and die off rather than surviving as they can on scored plastic
([Ak, Cliver & Kaspar 1994](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cutting-Boards-of-Plastic-and-Wood-Contaminated-Ak-Cliver/a6f94ab457ea5422a088808ebccc2f71aa563dc9 "Ak NO, Cliver DO, Kaspar CW (1994). Cutting Boards of Plastic and Wood Contaminated Experimentally with Bacteria. Journal of Food Protection 57:16–22.")).

*What to consider:* wood needs **care, not soaking** — don't leave it in water or run it
through hot dishwasher cycles, which cause cracking that *can* then harbor bacteria. With
bamboo, choose pieces made with food-safe adhesives, since some cheaper laminated bamboo
uses formaldehyde-based glues.

### Food-grade silicone — for anything touching non-stick

Food-grade silicone is heat-tolerant and gentle on coated surfaces, making it the right
choice for spatulas and stirring tools used in non-stick pans. It is far more stable than
the plastics it replaces.

*What to consider:* silicone is not perfectly inert — low-molecular-weight **siloxanes
migrate more with fat and heat**, and quality varies between products
([Liu et al. 2023](https://ift.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1541-4337.13165 "Liu Y et al. (2023). Progress in research on the safety of silicone rubber products in food processing. Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf 22(5):3955–3976.")).
Buy genuinely food-grade, post-cured silicone and replace any piece that feels gummy or
degraded.

> **In short:** stainless steel and solid wood cover almost every task with no coating to
> worry about; food-grade silicone is the one tool you reach for specifically to protect
> non-stick pans.

## Key Findings

- **Black plastic utensils can carry flame retardants from recycled electronics.** A 2024
  analysis of black plastic household items — including food-contact kitchen utensils —
  found banned and emerging brominated and organophosphate flame retardants in the large
  majority of bromine-positive samples, with e-waste recycling the likely source
  ([Liu, Brandsma & Schreder 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0045653524022173 "Liu M, Brandsma SH, Schreder E (2024). From e-waste to living space: flame retardants contaminating household items add to concern about plastic recycling. Chemosphere 365:143319. (See Dec 2024 corrigendum, PMID 39675991, correcting an exposure-dose calculation.)")).
  The authors later corrected an overstated exposure estimate, but the presence of these
  compounds in food-contact plastic is the durable finding.
- **Black nylon utensils can release primary aromatic amines.** Testing of food-contact
  materials found very high migration of primary aromatic amines (such as aniline and
  4,4′-methylenedianiline) from black nylon kitchenware into an acidic food simulant — in
  some samples far above the EU limit ([Trier et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20640962/ "Trier X et al. (2010). Primary aromatic amines (PAAs) in black nylon and other food-contact materials, 2004–2009. Food Addit Contam Part A 27.")).
- **Nylon migration rises with heat and contact time.** Polyamide utensils release caprolactam
  and oligomers increasingly as temperature and contact duration go up; risk-assessment
  guidance is to keep contact with hot food **as brief as possible** (on the order of a few
  minutes) ([BfR 2019](https://www.bfr.bund.de/cm/349/polyamide-kitchen-utensils-keep-contact-with-hot-food-as-brief-as-possible.pdf "German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR). Polyamide kitchen utensils: keep contact with hot food as brief as possible.")).
- **Wood is naturally antibacterial.** In classic hygiene testing, bacteria applied to wooden
  surfaces were absorbed and died off within minutes, while the same bacteria survived — and
  sometimes multiplied — on scratched plastic
  ([Ak, Cliver & Kaspar 1994](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cutting-Boards-of-Plastic-and-Wood-Contaminated-Ak-Cliver/a6f94ab457ea5422a088808ebccc2f71aa563dc9 "Ak NO, Cliver DO, Kaspar CW (1994). Cutting Boards of Plastic and Wood Contaminated Experimentally with Bacteria. Journal of Food Protection 57:16–22.")).
- **Food-grade silicone is stable within normal cooking temperatures,** with only minor
  siloxane migration that increases with fat and heat
  ([Liu et al. 2023](https://ift.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1541-4337.13165 "Liu Y et al. (2023). Progress in research on the safety of silicone rubber products in food processing. Compr Rev Food Sci Food Saf 22(5):3955–3976.")).

## Materials to Avoid + Risks

- **Black plastic utensils, especially in hot pans.** This is the highest-value swap on the
  page: black plastic is the category flagged for recycled-electronics flame-retardant
  contamination, and heat speeds any migration
  ([Liu, Brandsma & Schreder 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0045653524022173 "Liu M, Brandsma SH, Schreder E (2024). From e-waste to living space: flame retardants contaminating household items add to concern about plastic recycling. Chemosphere 365:143319.")).
- **Cheap nylon or melamine at high heat.** These can soften, degrade, or leach beyond their
  rated temperature; black nylon is the most studied offender
  ([Trier et al. 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20640962/ "Trier X et al. (2010). Primary aromatic amines (PAAs) in black nylon and other food-contact materials, 2004–2009. Food Addit Contam Part A 27.")).
- **Worn, melted, or gouged utensils.** Damaged surfaces are harder to clean and more likely
  to shed material — retire them.
- **Low-grade silicone.** Look for genuine food-grade silicone; a "pinch test" (the material
  turning white when twisted) can reveal filler in poor-quality products.

::: warning The one swap worth making
If you own **black plastic cooking utensils, replace them** with stainless steel, solid wood,
or food-grade silicone. It's the single clearest upgrade in this category — and an
inexpensive one.
:::

## Practical Tips

- Keep a few **stainless steel** workhorses (whisk, tongs, ladle); they're inert and last
  indefinitely.
- Use **silicone or wood** for anything that touches a hot non-stick pan, to protect both the
  coating and your food.
- **Dry wooden tools promptly** and never soak them or run them through high-heat dishwasher
  cycles — cracking is what lets a wooden tool harbor bacteria.
- Oil wooden utensils occasionally with a **food-safe finish** to extend their life.
- If you use nylon utensils, **don't leave them resting in hot pots**, and keep them off
  high, dry heat.
- Replace any plastic utensil that is **melted, scratched, or discolored.**
